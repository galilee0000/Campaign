Campaign 1

Awareness campaign to provide electricity to two million people in Cameroon

A nationwide campaign to win the trust of households and bring them on board with a programme connecting two million people to electricity.

Funded by
World Bank
Amount
US$25 million (revolving fund)
Country
Cameroon
Period
2022–2025

  1. Communication challenge

    Persuading households to sign up to the initiative at a time of widespread mistrust of government-led development programmes and a loss of confidence in the authorities.

  2. Objectives

    Provide electricity to two million people in Cameroon.

  3. Target audiences

    Households in rural, semi-urban and urban areas.

  4. My role

    Senior Campaign Manager.

  5. Strategic approach

    The campaign started with a pilot phase with a strong focus on gender, then was extended on a large scale.

  6. Creative concept

    The concept rests on a simple promise: “A household, an electric meter.” The goal was to make electricity feel concrete and within reach, not like a distant government project. In line with the gender focus of the pilot phase, women were the main voice of the campaign. The message was designed to spread the way news spreads in a neighbourhood: from one person to the next, in everyday language and with a touch of humour (“C’est le JAPAP de l’année !”). To overcome mistrust, the campaign put transparency first, showing who backs the programme, how repayments are tracked, who checks the work and where to complain. Every message ended with the same call to action: call the free number 8566 or register online.

  7. Channels used

    • Social media (Facebook)
    • WhatsApp community groups
    • Radio, including community radio
    • Television
    • Door-to-door and street outreach

  8. Dissemination approach

    We started in the pilot cities, then extended the campaign to the 687 localities covered by PERACE. On Facebook, I created and managed campaigns combining regular posts with paid ads aimed at households in the areas being connected. The same content was shared in WhatsApp groups and broadcast on television and on national and community radio. On the ground, our teams went door to door and out into the streets with flyers and leaflets, while billboards, banners and roll-ups kept the message visible in public places. Households could then sign up online or call 8566 with their questions or complaints.

  9. Measurable results

    More than 400,000 electrical connections were executed and around two million households were connected. On Facebook, 200,000 people reached by the campaign went on to contact the project.
